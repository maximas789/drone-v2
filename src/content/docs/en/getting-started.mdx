## What you need before you start

An email address, a Saudi mobile number, a valid identity document — national ID, iqama or GCC ID — and an aircraft, whoever built it.

<Callout kind="note">

No manufacturer serial number is asked for. A self-built aircraft and an FPV aircraft register here exactly like any other, and are issued a [Remote ID](/docs/remote-id) that stands in for the serial. That is the whole reason this system exists.

</Callout>

## 1 · Create an account

From **Create account**: your name, your email, a password. A confirmation email follows, and you can use the system before opening it — confirming your address is not a condition of signing in.

## 2 · Complete your pilot profile

The profile is three steps, and each one saves on its own, so you can close the page and come back:

1. **Name** — in full, in Arabic and in English, as written on your document.
2. **Identity** — document type, number and date of birth. You must be at least eighteen.
3. **Contact** — mobile number, city of residence, and an emergency number.

Only the **last four digits** of your document number are ever shown, including to you. The full number does not appear on any screen after you enter it.

<Callout kind="note">

**Identity is verified by a human reviewer.** Somebody on the team compares what you entered against your document and decides. There is no automatic verification anywhere in this system, and we send no text messages and no verification codes.

</Callout>

A complete profile is enough to **register an aircraft**. **Booking a flight** additionally requires that a reviewer has actually verified your identity.

## 3 · Register your aircraft

Five steps, from build type through to review and submit. The full detail is on [Registering a drone](/docs/registering-a-drone).

What happens after you submit:

- The application enters the **review queue** and is decided by a human reviewer. Approval is not instant.
- While it is under review it **cannot be edited or deleted** — what the reviewer decides has to be what the reviewer saw.
- On approval the aircraft is **issued a Remote ID** in the form `AJN-XXXX-XXXX`, and a QR code is printed from it and fixed to the airframe.
- On rejection you get a **written reason**, and the aircraft becomes editable again. Your data stays as it was; you do not start over.

Registration lasts **three years** from the date of issue.

## 4 · Book a flight window

This system is default-deny: flight is permitted only inside a published permitted zone, in a booked window. Read [Zones and rules](/docs/zones-and-rules), then [Booking a flight](/docs/booking-a-flight).

Before a single bookable slot appears, all of this has to be true:

| Requirement | Where it is met |
| --- | --- |
| A complete pilot profile | Step 2 |
| An identity a reviewer has verified | Step 2, then the reviewer's decision |
| An approved aircraft | Step 3 |
| An active Remote ID for that aircraft | Issued automatically on approval |
| Registration still in force | Three years from issue |

## Before you fly

- **Carry the Remote ID with you.** The aircraft's card is printable from its own page, and the code scans from any phone.
- **Check in during the window.** Check-in only works inside the slot's own time, and not checking in counts as a no-show.
- **Keep the aircraft in your line of sight** for the whole flight. You acknowledge this on every booking.

<Callout kind="warning">

A booking in this system does not replace any authorisation a competent authority requires.
