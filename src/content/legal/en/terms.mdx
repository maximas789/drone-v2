<H2 id="registering-an-aircraft">Registering an aircraft</H2>

An aircraft starts as a draft and is then submitted for review. After that:

- **The decision is discretionary and made by a human reviewer.** No aircraft
  registration is ever approved automatically.
- **A rejection comes with a written reason**, which you read as it was written.
  You may edit and resubmit, and there is no limit on attempts.
- **A reviewer cannot decide a submission they made themselves.** Separating the
  submitter from the approver is enforced in the code, not merely a policy.
- **Registration lasts 3 years** from issue, to the same calendar day three years
  on. After that it is expired and must be renewed.
- **Renewal does not change your Remote ID code.** A sticker already on your
  airframe keeps resolving after a renewal.

**No serial number is required.** Self-built and FPV aircraft register here with
a Remote ID in place of one — the gap this platform exists to close.

<H2 id="remote-id-obligations">Your Remote ID obligations</H2>

When a registration is approved, your aircraft is issued a **Remote ID code** and
a QR sticker. You agree to:

- Keep the sticker **visible and intact** on the aircraft.
- Never move one aircraft's code to another aircraft.
- Not fly on an **expired, suspended or revoked** registration.
- Keep the aircraft's details up to date if they change.

Anyone who scans the code sees the registration status and general details about
the aircraft **without seeing your identity**. What is and is not shown is set
out in the [privacy policy](/privacy#what-a-scan-reveals).

<H2 id="booking-a-flight">Booking a flight window</H2>

A booking allocates you a **seat** in a specific zone and time window. You agree
to:

- Fly within the zone's boundary, its time window, and its permitted ceiling.
- Follow the zone's rules and any published closure over it.
- Hold a registration that is **valid at the time of the flight**, not merely at
  the time of booking.

**Some zones approve bookings automatically and some do not.** Automatic
approval needs both conditions together: the zone must allow it, and your
no-show record must be clear — see [the next section](#cancellation-and-no-shows).
Where there is no automatic approval, the decision is discretionary and made by a
human reviewer, and the airspace is evaluated again before approval.

**An approved booking may be sent back to the review queue** if a zone boundary
moves beneath it. That is not a cancellation: your seat is held, and the decision
goes back to a person.

<H2 id="cancellation-and-no-shows">Cancellation and no-shows</H2>

| Case | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| You cancel | Allowed up to **2 hours** before the window starts, and not after |
| The operator cancels | **At any time**, with a written reason you receive |
| A published closure over the zone | The booking is cancelled automatically, whether approved or still pending |
| The aircraft's registration is revoked | All of its future bookings are cancelled |

The two hours are what makes a freed seat worth anything: a cancellation ten
minutes beforehand is a no-show with better manners.

A **no-show** is recorded if you do not check in and **30 minutes** pass after
the window ends.

<Callout kind="note">
  **What a no-show costs, in numbers:** **3 no-shows within 90 days** removes
  automatic approval, so your bookings go to a human reviewer instead.

  The window is **rolling**: there is no permanent penalty and nothing to apply
  to have lifted — as soon as the oldest no-show falls outside the 90 days,
  automatic approval returns on its own.
</Callout>

<H2 id="suspension-and-revocation">Suspension and revocation</H2>

An aircraft's registration may be revoked if:

- the information given was inaccurate or misleading;
- you flew outside your approved window, outside the zone's boundary, or against
  its rules;
- the Remote ID code was misused or moved to another aircraft;
- these terms were breached repeatedly.

**Revocation is an admin's power alone**, not a reviewer's, and **never happens
without a written reason**. It suspends the Remote ID and cancels every future
booking for that aircraft. Revocation is **reversible**: an admin can reinstate
the registration, and both acts stay in the log.

Every decision of this kind is written to a log that **cannot be edited or
deleted**.
